# Socratic Interrogation Handbook for Novel Brainstorming

This reference contains curated Socratic prompts designed to interrogate story concepts deeply without overwhelming the author. The agent should ask 1–2 questions per conversational turn.

---

## 1. Excavating the Protagonist's Ghost & Wound

Use these questions when an author has a character with cool skills or a job, but no emotional vulnerability:
- *"What is the memory your protagonist wakes up at 3:00 AM sweating about, wishing they could undo?"*
- *"What lie does your protagonist tell themselves every morning just to survive the day?"*
- *"If your protagonist walked into a room where someone truly loved them unconditionally, what would make them turn around and run away in terror?"*
- *"Who was the person that broke your protagonist's faith in the world, and what lesson did your protagonist wrongly learn from that betrayal?"*

---

## 2. Uncovering the Central Thematic Argument

Theme is not a trite moral; it is an unresolved philosophical debate:
- *"What is a belief you held passionately ten years ago that you now realize is deeply flawed?"*
- *"If your two main characters had an argument at 2:00 AM over whiskey, where both of them are 100% morally justified from their perspective, what are they yelling about?"*
- *"What is the cost of victory in this story? What noble virtue must the protagonist sacrifice to achieve their goal?"*
- *"Does the preservation of order justify the silencing of truth? Or does the pursuit of absolute freedom inevitably birth chaos?"*

---

## 3. High-Concept Hook Triangulation ("X Meets Y with a Twist")

Use these prompts when an author's concept feels too familiar or generic:
- *"If we took the core engine of [Famous Story A] and collided it into the setting and atmospheric tone of [Famous Story B], what unexpected sparks fly?"*
- *"What is the single rule of reality in your world that, if broken for five seconds, turns society upside down?"*
- *"What is the ordinary, everyday problem your protagonist has that becomes terrifyingly complicated by their magical or sci-fi environment?"*

---

## 4. Emotional Destination & Ending Catharsis

Before plotting Act 2, establish the emotional target of the final sentence:
- *"When the reader closes the book and sets it on their nightstand, what is the physical sensation in their chest? Do they feel gut-punched with tragic grief, exhilarated with fist-pumping joy, or chilled by a lingering question?"*
- *"In the final chapter, does the protagonist get what they Wanted, or what they Needed? What does it cost them to choose?"*
