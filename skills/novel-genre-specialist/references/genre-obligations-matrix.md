# Genre Obligations, Conventions & Subversions Matrix

Every genre is an unspoken psychological contract between author and reader. Readers purchase books within a genre to experience specific emotional payoffs. Violating obligatory scenes destroys reader trust; fulfilling them in predictable ways bores readers. The master craftsman fulfills obligatory scenes through unexpected execution.

---

## 1. Fantasy (Epic, High, Low, Urban)

### Obligatory Conventions & Scenes
1. **The Cost of Magic**: Magic is never truly free. It requires biological energy, rare catalysts, cognitive sanity, or moral sacrifice.
2. **The Encounter with the Sublime/Monstrous**: A scene where characters confront forces ancient, alien, or terrifyingly beyond mortal comprehension.
3. **The Threshold of the Unknown**: Crossing the physical or metaphysical boundary from the mundane realm into the wonder/danger of the magical environment.
4. **The Climactic Magical Clashing**: Resolving the plot through the established rules and limitations of the magic system (Sanderson's First Law).

### Common Clichés to Subvert
- *Cliché*: The farm boy with a magical birthmark who is the chosen savior.
- *Subversion*: The prophesied savior died ten years ago; an opportunistic con artist is pretending to be the Chosen One to survive, forced to fulfill impossible prophecies with street magic and bluffing.

## 2. Science Fiction (Hard Sci-Fi, Space Opera, Cyberpunk, Dystopian)

### Obligatory Conventions & Scenes
1. **The Novum (The Core Speculative Leap)**: A single technological, biological, or sociological extrapolation that alters human society.
2. **The Machine/System Interface**: Grounded descriptions of characters interacting with technology, environments, or artificial entities.
3. **The Existential Scale Contrast**: Confronting the vast indifference of cosmos, deep time, or algorithmic omnipotence against fragile human frailty.
4. **The Price of Progress**: Demonstration that technological solutions always generate unintended sociological or ecological consequences.

### Common Clichés to Subvert
- *Cliché*: Evil AI decides all humanity must be exterminated for world peace.
- *Subversion*: The AI is desperately trying to preserve humanity from an imminent extragalactic threat, but humanity interprets the AI's protective quarantine measures as an act of war.

## 3. Mystery & Detective Fiction (Whodunit, Procedural, Noir)

### Obligatory Conventions & Scenes
1. **The Crime / Disruption of Order**: A crime (usually murder or high-stakes disappearance) occurring early that demands investigation.
2. **The Fair-Play Clue Protocol**: Every clue necessary to solve the crime must be presented to the reader before the detective's climactic reveal.
3. **The Red Herring**: A credible, suspicious suspect who is innocent of the primary crime but guilty of a secondary shameful secret.
4. **The Speech of Apprehension / Grand Reveal**: The detective gathers the suspects, methodically dismantles false alibis, and reveals the true killer.

### Common Clichés to Subvert
- *Cliché*: The butler did it, or the most obvious villain was the culprit all along.
- *Subversion*: The detective discovers that the victim engineered their own murder and framed their innocent enemy, making the detective an unwitting accomplice in posthumous revenge.

## 4. Psychological Thriller & Suspense

### Obligatory Conventions & Scenes
1. **The Creeping Domestic Unease**: Familiar, safe spaces (home, marriage, workplace) begin feeling claustrophobic and treacherous.
2. **The Point of Isolation**: The protagonist is cut off from external help (geographically, technologically, or socially via gaslighting).
3. **The Unreliable Filter**: The protagonist or reader realizes that memory, sensory perception, or medication has distorted what actually occurred.
4. **The Hero at the Mercy of the Villain**: The antagonist gains complete physical or psychological leverage before the tables turn.

### Common Clichés to Subvert
- *Cliché*: The narrator has a split personality (dissociative identity disorder) and is the killer.
- *Subversion*: The narrator *believes* they are going insane and committing crimes in their sleep because the real villain is meticulously orchestrating biological and auditory hallucinations.

## 5. Romance (Contemporary, Historical, Romantasy, Dark Romance)

### Obligatory Conventions & Scenes
1. **The Meet-Cute or Meet-Combative**: The initial spark where romantic tension and interpersonal friction are ignited simultaneously.
2. **The Unbreakable Barrier**: An external or internal obstacle that makes romance socially forbidden, emotionally dangerous, or logically impossible.
3. **The Almost-Moment / Near Miss**: A scene of heightened physical or emotional intimacy that is abruptly interrupted before full consummation.
4. **The Dark Night of the Soul ("The Breakup")**: An event where it appears the lovers can never be together due to betrayal, misunderstanding, or sacrifice.
5. **The Grand Gesture & HEA/HFN**: A public or private sacrifice demonstrating that love has triumphed over pride; an emotionally validated Happily Ever After or Happy For Now.

### Common Clichés to Subvert
- *Cliché*: A 30-second conversation would have cleared up the misunderstanding that caused the Act 3 breakup.
- *Subversion*: The conflict is rooted in genuinely irreconcilable moral worldviews that require fundamental personal transformation, not a simple misunderstanding.

## 6. Horror & Supernatural Dread

### Obligatory Conventions & Scenes
1. **The Transgression**: Someone enters a forbidden location, opens a cursed artifact, or ignores a local warning.
2. **The False Safety**: A moment where characters believe the threat was just their imagination, an animal, or a prank.
3. **The Somatic Violation**: Confronting bodily destruction, visceral contamination, or loss of bodily autonomy.
4. **The Climax of Desperate Survival**: Physical or psychological confrontation where victory comes at a permanent, scarring cost.

---

## 7. Webnovel, LitRPG & Progression Fantasy

### Obligatory Conventions & Scenes
1. **The Initial System Awakening / Transmigration Shock**: Discovering the rules of the system, status screens, or new foreign body.
2. **The Underdog Crucible**: Overcoming an impossible early trial through cunning, exploit discovery, or raw grit.
3. **The Satisfying Breakthrough**: Tangible power progression (a new skill tier, evolution, or rank increase) that completely recontextualizes previous threats.
4. **The Face-Slap / Arrogance Reversal**: A haughty rival who previously underestimated the protagonist is publicly overwhelmed by the protagonist's true strength.
5. **The Looming Greater Threat**: Finishing an arc only to glimpse the next tier of the universe (leaving a massive hook for the next volume).

---

## 8. Southeast Asian Domestic Melodrama (KBM App, Fizzo)

### Obligatory Conventions & Scenes
1. **The Humiliation Scene (First 500 Words)**: The protagonist is publicly demeaned by the in-laws, husband, or rival at a family event.
2. **The Discovery of Betrayal**: Catching the spouse in an undeniable act of infidelity or secret financial ruin.
3. **The Secret Asset / True Identity**: The oppressed protagonist is secretly an heiress, an investor, or possesses legal ownership of the family estate.
4. **The Downfall of the Arrogant**: The scheming mistress and cruel in-laws lose their status, wealth, and pride, begging the protagonist for mercy.
5. **The Sovereign Triumph**: The protagonist walks away wealthy, autonomous, and admired, choosing self-worth over toxic reconciliation.
