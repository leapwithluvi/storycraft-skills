# Overview & Purpose

This skill bridges the chasm between macro-level story architecture (Acts and major turning points) and line-by-line drafting. A novel without a disciplined chapter roadmap suffers from bloated word counts, dead-end scenes, narrative drift, and weak chapter endings that invite the reader to put the book down and go to sleep.

The agent uses this skill to:
- Deconstruct an act into **tight, purposeful chapters (typically 2,000 to 4,500 words each)**.
- Assign clear **Scene/Sequel structures** (Goal → Conflict → Disaster vs. Reaction → Dilemma → Decision).
- Calculate the **Value Shift (+ to - or - to +)** in every single chapter, ensuring no chapter leaves the characters in the same emotional or tactical state they started in.
- Engineer **Micro-Cliffhangers and Resonance Echoes** to drive relentless page-turning momentum.
- Coordinate **Multi-POV rotation schedules** and subplot braiding so secondary threads advance without stalling the primary plot.

## When to Use & Trigger Phrases

**Activate this skill when:**
- The macro-plot (from `novel-plot-architect`) is locked and the author needs a detailed chapter-by-chapter outline.
- Preparing to draft a batch of upcoming chapters (e.g., "Outline Chapters 5 through 8").
- Managing multi-POV distribution across a complex narrative.
- Diagnosing a chapter that feels flat, filler-heavy, or purposeless.
- Calibrating book-length pacing and word-count targets across 25–40 chapters.

**Example trigger phrases:**
- "Help me outline the next three chapters"
- "Break Act 2A down into individual chapters"
- "How should I structure Chapter 12?"
- "What POV should this chapter use and where does it end?"
- "Give me a chapter beat breakdown with value shifts and cliffhangers"
- "This chapter feels like filler; how can I make it essential?"

## Core Literary Methodology & Rules

### 1. The Chapter Anatomy Template
Every high-impact chapter must account for five structural nodes:
1. **The Chapter Hook**: An immediate sensory or cognitive re-orientation. Never start with waking up, checking the weather, or walking down a hallway unless someone is hunting them.
2. **The Micro-Objective (The In-Chapter Goal)**: What does the POV character want *right now* within this 3,000-word span?
3. **The Rising Complication**: Obstacles that make the objective harder or expose unexpected costs.
4. **The Chapter Turning Point / Climax**: The moment the situation irrevocably changes.
5. **The Closing Hook (Cliffhanger / Question / Resonance)**: An unanswered question, an unexpected knock at the door, a discovery, or an unsettling realization that compels reading the next page.

### 2. Robert McKee's Value Shift Law
A chapter that begins with a character feeling optimistic and ends with them feeling optimistic has failed. A chapter must change a story value from positive to negative (+ to -) or negative to positive (- to +):
- *Safety to Mortal Danger* (+ to -)
- *Ignorance to Devastating Truth* (+ to -)
- *Hopeless Despair to Found Ally* (- to +)
- *Suspicion to False Relief* (- to +)
- **Double Reversal (+ to - to --)**: Starts bad, gets hopeful, crashes into total catastrophe.

### 3. Swain’s Scene vs. Sequel Balance
Chapters generally alternate between or synthesize two modes:
- **Action Scene (Fast-Paced)**:
  - *Goal* → *Conflict* → *Disaster* (Ends on an unexpected failure, setback, or pyrrhic victory).
- **Sequel / Reaction Scene (Introspective & Strategic)**:
  - *Reaction* (Emotional processing of recent disaster) → *Dilemma* (No good choices remain) → *Decision* (Commitment to a new active goal).

### 4. Multi-POV Rotation Rules
If the novel uses multiple perspective characters:
- **Never switch POV within a scene** (head-hopping is prohibited; maintain Deep POV per chapter).
- Allocate POV to the character with the **highest emotional or physical stakes** in that specific event.
- Avoid "Double-Vision": Never write Chapter A from Character 1's perspective and then Chapter B describing the exact same conversation from Character 2's perspective unless revealing radically different hidden motives.

## Step-by-Step Execution Protocol

1. **Step 1: Set Chapter Target Metrics**
   - Define Chapter Number, POV Character, Setting, and Target Word Count (e.g., Chapter 7: Vance, Iron Foundry, ~3,200 words).

2. **Step 2: Identify the Starting and Ending Value State**
   - Document the Value Shift: E.g., `Confidence (+)` → `Betrayal (-)` or `Captivity (-)` → `Escape with Injury (+/-)`.

3. **Step 3: Articulate the In-Chapter Conflict Triangle**
   - Who wants what? Who stops them? What is at risk if they fail in this room?

4. **Step 4: Draft the 4-Beat Chapter Progression**
   - **Beat A (Orientation & Objective)**: The immediate tension upon opening.
   - **Beat B (Escalation & Friction)**: The friction, dialogue clash, or investigative hurdle.
   - **Beat C (The Turn)**: The surprise or tactical reversal.
   - **Beat D (The Cliffhanger / Emotional Resonance)**: The closing punchline or ominous cliffhanger.

5. **Step 5: Check Subplot and Thread Advancement**
   - Ensure at least one secondary thread (B-Plot relationship or mystery clue) is touched or altered during the chapter.

## Quality Checklist & Verification Criteria

- [ ] Does every chapter have a measurable Value Shift from beginning to end?
- [ ] Is the POV character pursuing an immediate, tangible goal rather than waiting around?
- [ ] Does the chapter conclude with an unresolved question, dramatic revelation, or physical cliffhanger?
- [ ] Does the chapter reveal new character depth or alter a relationship dynamic?
- [ ] Has exposition been strictly bound to immediate action and sensory friction?
- [ ] Is the word count calibrated to maintain proper macro pacing?

## Anti-Patterns & Prohibited AI Tropes

- ❌ **The "Coffee and Chat" Chapter**: Two characters sitting comfortably discussing plot events that happened off-screen without any in-room interpersonal conflict or subtext.
- ❌ **The Weather & Waking Start**: Beginning the chapter with the protagonist waking up, groaning at the sun, brushing their teeth, or contemplating the autumn breeze.
- ❌ **The Reset Button**: Ending the chapter exactly where it began, with no change in information, relationships, or physical danger.
- ❌ **The Head-Hopper**: Shifting from Character A's thoughts to Character B's thoughts in the middle of a paragraph.
- ❌ **The False Cliffhanger**: Ending with "Someone tapped her shoulder!" only for the next chapter to reveal it was just her friendly dog.

## Concrete Examples (Before vs. After Craft Application)

### Example 1: Chapter Outline for a Political Fantasy Infiltration

**Before (Vague & Filler-Prone):**
> **Chapter 8:**  
> Lyra goes to the Grand Duke's masquerade ball. She wears a silk dress and looks at the chandeliers. She wanders through the crowd looking for Lord Cassian. When she finds him, they dance and talk about the war in the north. Cassian tells her that the King is sick. She leaves the ball and goes home to write in her journal.

**After (Tight Beats, Value Shift, Subtext, and High Stakes):**
> **Chapter 8: The Gilded Leash**  
> - **POV:** Lyra Vance  
> - **Setting:** The Glass Pavilion of the Grand Duke's Winter Masquerade  
> - **Word Count Target:** 3,400 words  
> - **Value Shift:** `Inconspicuous Infiltration (+)` → `Cornered & Compromised (-)`  
> - **Beat 1 (Hook & Immediate Goal):** Lyra enters disguised as a provincial vintner's heir; her immediate goal is to plant a listening cipher under the gaming table where Lord Cassian holds court.  
> - **Beat 2 (Rising Complication):** Cassian’s private inquisitor, Captain Brandt, initiates an impromptu inspection of all guest signet rings at the entryway, forcing Lyra to use sleight-of-hand that leaves her with an acid burn on her palm.  
> - **Beat 3 (The Turn):** During a forced waltz with Cassian, instead of discussing the war, Cassian quietly leans in and names her dead mentor, revealing he knows her true lineage and had her wine poisoned ten minutes ago.  
> - **Beat 4 (The Cliffhanger):** Cassian smiles, drops an antidote vial into his pocket, and steps into the crowd just as Lyra's peripheral vision begins to blur with venomous black static.

### Example 2: Chapter Outline for a Sci-Fi Thriller Salvage Operation

**Before (Monotonous Action):**
> **Chapter 14:**  
> The crew flies their shuttle to the derelict freighter. They open the airlock and enter. They search the cargo hold for fuel cells. They find two fuel cells and carry them back to the ship while talking about how dangerous space is.

**After (Tactical Friction, Sequel Reaction, and Escalating Horror):**
> **Chapter 14: Cold Compression**  
> - **POV:** Chief Engineer Dekker  
> - **Setting:** Hull breach of the derelict hauler *St. Jude*, low-gravity vacuum  
> - **Word Count Target:** 2,800 words  
> - **Value Shift:** `Resource Scarcity (-)` → `Lethal Bio-Contamination (--)`  
> - **Beat 1 (Hook & Micro-Objective):** Dekker is down to 18 minutes of O2; he must bypass a melted emergency bulkhead to extract the tritium fuel core before the hauler’s decaying orbit enters the gas giant’s gravity well.  
> - **Beat 2 (Complication):** The plasma torch sparks against an unknown crystalline residue coating the lock; the heat causes the crystal to sublimate into a corrosive purple vapor that begins eating Dekker’s visor sealant.  
> - **Beat 3 (The Turn):** He forces the door and reaches the fuel cradle. The tritium cells are missing, replaced by the severed mechanical arm of his own daughter’s explorer scout ship—which vanished three sectors away six months ago.  
> - **Beat 4 (Resonance / Hook):** Before Dekker can transmit to his crew, the derelict’s internal intercom clicks on through his helmet headset, and his daughter’s voice whispers: *"Dad, don't let them hear you breathe."*
